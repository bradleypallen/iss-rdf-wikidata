# The full run over spouse (P26), as of 14 November 2026

Dated note, Saturday 14 November 2026. All times are UTC. Everything in the run is as of
the snapshot, **2026-11-14T17:51:57+00:00**. `entries/entries.json` was used as it stood (two rules,
nine entries); nothing in it was changed for this run.

Statements are marked by what they rest on: **live** (the snapshot), **checker**
(Wikidata's constraint checker through its API), **inferred**, or **ours**.

## 1. Snapshot

* **Source.** The QLever endpoint `https://qlever.dev/api/wikidata`, run by the University of Freiburg.
  It describes its data as "Complete Wikidata, TTL from https://dumps.wikimedia.org/wikidatawiki/entities
  (latest-all.ttl.bz2 from 10.08.2026 munged ...), updated LIVE". It is a live copy, not a
  frozen file, and it exposes no "updated to" time.
* **One query.** `queries/full-p26-snapshot.rq`, a single CONSTRUCT, started
  17:51:57 and took 18.416 s. It returned 3,049,566 triples
  (419 MB, sha256 `308de3ce06210761…`), with no triple repeated.
* **Consistency.** A count of direct spouse triples on the same endpoint gave 987,188 at
  17:51:56 and 987,188 at 17:52:16; the snapshot holds 987,188.
* **What it holds.**

| Triples | Count |
|---|---:|
| spouse (P26), every direct triple | 987,188 |
| instance of (P31), of every subject and value | 926,506 |
| date of birth (P569), of every subject and value | 616,339 |
| date of death (P570), of every subject and value | 515,382 |
| subclass of (P279), every triple at or above a class of a party | 4,149 |
| the symmetric constraint statement on P26 (no exception listed) | 2 |

* **Unknown values** arrive as Skolem IRIs under `.well-known/genid/`, as on the Wikidata
  query service; there are no blank nodes.
* **Direct triples only.** These are best-rank ("truthy") triples. Section 5 shows what
  that hides.
* The endpoint cuts an uncompressed response at about 4 MB; the query runner asks for gzip.
* The snapshot and the subset built from it are too large for git (about 400 MB each).
  Their checksums are in `results/full-p26-snapshot/*.meta.json` and
  `data/full-p26/PROVENANCE.json`.

Why this source and not a dump: Wikimedia's truthy dump of 11 November 2026 is 43.7 GB
compressed and takes hours to fetch and filter; whole-property queries time out on the
Wikidata query service. Data retrieved from Wikidata is in the public domain under CC0 1.0.

## 2. The subset and the class hierarchy

`data/full-p26/asserted.nt`, 3,046,484 triples, built by
`tools/build_full_subset.py`. The full class hierarchy is not materialized: of the
4,149 subclass triples fetched,
1,067 are kept, those on a path to one of the

That this loses nothing an entry reads was checked on two samples of 200
spouse parties (seed 20261114), by running the pipeline with the kept
subclass triples and again with all 4,149

## 5. Calibration against the checker

Up to 50 spouse triples were drawn from each of twelve categories (seed 20261114,
`tools/sample_for_checker.py`; `data/full-p26/calibration-sample.json`). The statement
behind each triple was looked up on QLever, and the checker was asked about those
statements through `wbcheckconstraints`, at 18:02:31 to 18:03:20, about ten
minutes after the snapshot. For each statement two sets of constraint types are compared:

* **entries**: the symmetric constraint when the reverse triple is a new default
  conclusion, and the constraint of each incompatibility entry that applies at K;
* **checker**: the constraints reported as violation, warning or suggestion.

A conflict over Cons has no counterpart in the checker and is not part of the comparison.
All statements asked about were returned; none was deprecated.

| Category | Population | Statements checked | Entries expect | Same as entries | Entries only | Checker only |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| reciprocated | 969,652 | 50 | nothing: 50 | 50 | none | none |
| clean-default | 12,484 | 50 | symmetric: 50 | 46 | symmetric: 4 | none |
| conflict-conflicts-with | 229 | 50 | symmetric: 50 | 50 | none | none |
| conflict-subject-type | 418 | 50 | none-of, symmetric: 4; subject type, symmetric: 3; symmetric: 43 | 43 | symmetric: 5 | contemporary: 2 |
| conflict-contemporary | 55 | 50 | contemporary, symmetric: 50 | 35 | contemporary: 12; symmetric: 5 | none |
| unknown-value | 2,661 | 99 | symmetric: 99 | 0 | symmetric: 99 | none |
| contemporary-no-1-january | 75 | 50 | contemporary: 42; contemporary, symmetric: 8 | 44 | contemporary: 4; symmetric: 2 | none |
| contemporary-1-january | 1,435 | 50 | contemporary: 50 | 27 | contemporary: 23 | none |
| contemporary-several-dates | 113 | 51 | contemporary: 49; contemporary, symmetric: 2 | 0 | contemporary: 51 | none |
| subject-type-at-k | 135 | 50 | subject type: 39; subject type, symmetric: 11 | 50 | none | none |

The unknown-value category has 99 statements for 50 triples, because a subject with one
unknown-value spouse statement often has several and an unknown value is a different term
on the statement than on the direct triple.

**Where the two are the same.** Reciprocated pairs, none of 50 flagged. Subject type at K,
50 of 50. None-of at K, 22 of 22. Name-item values whose default conclusion is in conflict
with conflicts-with: the checker reports the symmetric constraint only, 50 of 50.

**Where they differ, and why** (`tools/calibration_followup.py`, lookups on QLever at about 18:05)

| Symmetric violation expected by the entries, none reported | Statements |
|---|---:|
| a statement y spouse x exists, not at best rank (DeprecatedRank) | 5 |
| a statement y spouse x exists, not at best rank (NormalRank) | 4 |
| y is a redirect, and its target has the reverse statement | 5 |

| Dates the contemporary entry compared | Checker reports contemporary | Checker does not |
|---|---:|---:|
| every compared pair has a date coarser than a day (century at best) | 31 | 32 |
| every compared pair has a date coarser than a day (decade at best) | 0 | 1 |
| every compared pair has a date coarser than a day (millennium at best) | 0 | 1 |

1. **Unknown values** (checker). On none of 99 statements does the checker report the
   symmetric constraint; the entry draws a conclusion for each.
2. **Rank and redirects** (live). The entries expected a symmetric violation on 14 sampled
   spouse triples where the checker reported none. In all 14 a reverse statement exists that
   the direct triples do not show: on 9 it is not at best rank, and on 5 the value is a
   redirect whose target has it. In the clean-default category that is 4 of 50.
3. **Several dates** (checker). On none of 51 statements where only some death precedes
   some birth does the checker report contemporary.
4. **Date precision** (checker; the reading is inferred). Where a compared pair of dates is
   exact to the day the checker reports contemporary on 53 of 57 statements. Where the best
   pair has a century-precision date it does on 31 of 63. The four day-exact statements
   it does not report are not explained.
5. **Conflicts-with and subclass-of** (checker). Of the six spouse triples to which
   conflicts-with applies at K, the checker reports the three where a forbidden class is a
   direct class of the subject and none of the three reached only through subclass-of.
6. **Checker only.** On 2 statements the checker reports contemporary and the entries do
   not. Not looked into; the entries compare date of birth and date of death only.

## 6. What rests on what

* **Live, as of the snapshot:** every count in section 4.
* **Checker, on samples of at most 50:** section 5. The sample proportions are not
  estimates for the whole population with any stated error.
* **Inferred:** that the checker takes date precision into account; that it resolves a
  redirected value before looking for the reverse statement.
* **Ours:** reading a symmetric-constraint violation as a default consequence; the subject
  type entry as "incompatible unless typed"; applying the default to unknown values; the
  conflicts over Cons.
* The counts are over best-rank direct triples. No earlier published count is compared
  with them here.

## 7. Open points

1. **Missing reverse over direct triples overstates.** Some reverse statements exist at
   another rank or on a redirect's target. Reading spouse statements at all ranks, and
   resolving redirects, would need the statement nodes and `owl:sameAs` in the snapshot.
2. **Unknown values.** 2,661 conclusions,
   all in conflict with subject type; the checker reports nothing on them.
3. **Contemporary.** 1,689 of
   1,771 instances at K compare a date that falls on 1 January,
   and 60
   of 859 party pairs fail only the
   weaker comparison. As written the entries are stricter than the checker on both counts.
4. **Conflicts-with.** With the regime's subclass rule the entry is stricter than the checker.
5. **Deposit.** The snapshot is not in git. It would go into the archived release.
